Check your resume bullets, one rule at a time

Paste the bullets from one job, one per line, and get five plain checks on each. It is free.

Nothing is uploaded or stored. The checks run in this page, in your browser. Your text is not sent to a server, not saved, and not logged, and this site sets no cookies. Close the tab and it is gone.

Bullet marks such as a hyphen, asterisk or number are removed for you. Paste the bullets of one job together so the tense check has something to compare.

What this is, and what it is not

This is a set of five fixed rules run over text you paste. Each one is a heuristic: a pattern, a word list or a count with a cutoff I chose for ordinary one or two line bullets. Each result has a short explanation of exactly what the rule looks at, under "How this rule works".

It cannot tell whether a bullet is true. It cannot know your job, what you were proud of, or which number matters to the employer you are writing to. A bullet can pass all five checks and still say nothing useful, and a bullet can trip two and still be the right one. Use the results as a list of bullets to reread, and change a bullet only to something that is true.

It does not simulate an applicant tracking system. Nobody outside an employer can say how theirs reads a bullet.

The five checks

  • Opening verb. Flags starts such as "responsible for", "duties included" and "worked on". Passes a list of action verbs and any other word ending in -ed.
  • Number or outcome. Looks for a digit, a dollar or percent sign, a number word, or a verb that names a result. It never asks you to invent a number.
  • Length. Words per bullet. Eight to thirty is treated as fine.
  • Vague words. A fixed list of words and phrases such as "various", "stuff" and "team player".
  • Tense consistency. Compares the tense of each bullet's first verb with the majority across the bullets you pasted.

Known limits: the verb list is finite, so a real action verb it does not know gets a "look" and not a "fine". The tense rule reads only the first word and cannot tell a current job from a finished one. Numbers are detected, not judged. The cutoffs and the vague word list are my judgment. The Purdue OWL resume sections page advises specific action verbs, past tense for earlier jobs and present tense for a current one, and the same structure across bullets. Nothing else here comes from an outside standard. For how to find the facts behind a bullet, see turning duties into bullets, and for how many to write, how many bullets per job.
