Cover letter structure that fits on one page

Updated 21 October 2026

A cover letter has one job: to connect your resume to this particular role in a way a bullet list cannot. It is where you can say why this employer, and tell one short story that shows you can do the work. It is not a second resume in paragraph form.

Most letters that go wrong fail in the same way. They are interchangeable. Swap the company name and nothing else needs to change. A good structure makes that harder, because every part has to point at something specific.

The parts, in order

1. Heading

Your name, email and phone, the date, and the employer's name and address if you have it. If you are pasting the letter into an online form, you can skip the address block and start at the greeting. Keep your contact details identical to the ones on your resume.

2. Greeting

Use a name if you can find one. The posting, the company's team page or a professional networking profile of the hiring manager will sometimes give it. If you cannot find one after a few minutes of looking, "Dear Hiring Team" is perfectly acceptable. Do not guess a name and do not use "To whom it may concern" if you can avoid it.

3. Opening paragraph: two or three sentences

Name the exact role and where you saw it. Then give one reason you are a fit, stated plainly, and one reason this employer interests you. The reason should be about them, not their size or reputation. Something specific you read on their site, a product you use, a project they described in the posting.

Skip openers about how excited you are or how much you have always admired the company. They tell the reader nothing they can check.

4. Body: one or two paragraphs, one point each

Pick the one or two requirements from the posting that you can best support, and give each its own paragraph. Purdue's writing lab gives the same advice: focus each body paragraph on one qualification, and describe where and how you developed it rather than just listing skills. State the point in the first sentence, tell the short story of when you did it, then connect it back to what the job needs.

Keep the details consistent with your resume. If the letter says you ran a team of five and the resume says four, the reader will notice. And do not just restate the resume. Point to it with a sentence like "the marketing club role on my resume involved this," and spend the paragraph on the part a bullet cannot hold: what you were trying to do and what happened.

5. Closing paragraph: two sentences

Say you would like to talk more about the role, mention when you are available if that is useful, and thank them for reading. Then sign off with "Sincerely" or "Best regards" and your name. Do not apologize for anything, and do not demand a reply.

How long

Under one page, in a normal-sized font. Three or four short paragraphs usually lands there. If you are over, cut the second body paragraph before you shrink the font.

An example, built from invented facts

This sample is made up. The person, the company and the numbers do not exist. It is here to show the structure.

Example, invented for illustration 
Dear Ms. Alvarez,

I am applying for the Marketing Analyst Intern role at Trailhead Supply that I found on your careers page. I have spent two semesters tracking which posts get replies for a student club, and your posting's focus on understanding which campaigns bring customers back is the part of marketing I most want to do more of.

As social media chair of my university's marketing club, I managed posts across three accounts. Each Friday I went through which posts had gotten the most replies and changed the next week's plan to match. That is a small version of the weekly reporting your posting describes, and it is where I learned that a simple spreadsheet, checked every week, answers more questions than a complicated one checked once.

I have not used Tableau yet. I have used Excel for weekly sales tracking at my part-time retail job, and I am taking a data visualization course this term. I expect to learn the tool quickly, and I would be glad to show you the tracker I built.

Thank you for reading. I am free to talk any afternoon next week.

Sincerely,
Jordan Example

Notice what the example does. It names the role and the source. It picks two points and gives each a small, true story. It states the Tableau gap in one plain sentence instead of hiding it. A reader learns more from that one honest line than from a paragraph about passion for data.

Before you send

  • Read it aloud. Anything stiff or repetitive gets cut.
  • Check the employer's name and the role title against the posting, letter by letter.
  • Check that every claim also appears on your resume, or that you can speak to it in an interview.
  • Delete any sentence that could appear unchanged in a letter to a different company.

If you use an AI tool to draft a letter, give it only true facts about yourself and check every sentence it returns. It can invent details to make a paragraph sound complete. A letter with one invented detail is worse than one with a plain gap.
