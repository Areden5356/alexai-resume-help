Reference and employment gap questions, answered honestly

Updated 21 October 2026

Two questions make people nervous late in an application: who can vouch for you, and why there is a stretch of time with nothing on the page. Both have simple honest answers. The trick is to prepare them before anyone asks.

References

What the source says

Purdue's writing lab describes a reference sheet as a list of people who can speak to your work and character, and notes that employers often ask for one even when a resume says references are available on request. It advises choosing people who know your work well and can describe your strengths accurately, such as past employers, professors and advisors. It says to get each person's agreement before you list them, and to give each a copy of your resume and talk through the job so they can speak to it. For format, it suggests your name and addresses at the top, then for each reference the name, organization or department, title, address, phone number, and a short note on how you know them.

Practical steps

  1. Ask first. Ask in a message that makes it easy to say no: "Would you be comfortable being a reference for internship applications this fall?" A person who hesitates is not a good reference.
  2. Send them material. Your current resume, the kind of roles you are applying for, and one or two things you did that they saw. They should not have to guess.
  3. Tell them about each application when a posting is likely to lead to a call. A short note with the employer and role keeps them ready.
  4. Keep a separate sheet. Do not put references on the resume itself. Have the sheet ready as its own page, with your contact details at the top.
  5. Thank them, and tell them how it turned out.

If you have little work history, a professor, a club adviser or a volunteer coordinator can be a good choice, since they have seen you do real work. Avoid listing friends and relatives as references for a job.

Gaps in your history

A gap is a stretch where the resume shows no job or school. Reasons vary: caring for family, illness, a move, a job search that took a while, travel, a layoff. Employers ask about gaps because unexplained spaces invite guesses. Short, truthful answers close the question.

Principles

  • Tell the truth, briefly. You do not owe your whole story. You owe an accurate one. Do not stretch dates to hide a gap. A date that does not match what a former employer would say is a problem you created for yourself.
  • Say what the time was, then what you do now. Two sentences: what happened, then what you are ready for.
  • Skip health and family detail you do not want to share. "I took time away for family reasons and I am ready to work full time" is enough. I am not a lawyer, and what an employer may ask varies by place, so check local rules or ask an advisor if a question seems out of bounds.
  • Include real work done in the gap. Volunteering, a course, freelance tasks, a project. List them with dates, as you would a job, only if they were real.

Where to address it

On the resume, honest dates are enough. Using years instead of months for older jobs is a layout choice some people make, as long as the years are accurate. In the cover letter, add a sentence only when the gap is large or recent. Otherwise save the explanation for the interview, if it is asked.

Sample wording, invented

Example, invented for illustration 
Asked in an interview: "I see nothing between June and January."

Answer: "I took that time to care for a family member. That is
settled now. During those months I finished an online
bookkeeping course, and I am looking for a full time role where
I can use it."

Asked: "Why did you leave your last job?"

Answer: "The store closed. I had worked there two years, and I
am looking for a role with more responsibility."

These are samples of shape. Do not borrow the facts. If you did not take a course, the answer must not mention one. Your answer is only as good as it is true.

If a reference cannot be reached

People move and change email addresses. Before a major application round, check that each contact detail still works. If you lose touch with someone, replace them rather than list a number that rings out.

A short checklist

  • Three people have said yes and have your resume.
  • Your reference sheet is a separate page, with their details checked.
  • You can explain any gap in two sentences that are true.
  • Dates on the resume match what a former employer would say.
