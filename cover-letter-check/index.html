What this is, and what it is not

This is a set of nine fixed rules run over text you paste. Each one is a heuristic: a pattern or a count with a cutoff I chose for ordinary one page letters. Each result has a short explanation of exactly what the rule looks at, under "How this rule works".

It does not judge whether your letter is good. It cannot know what the hiring manager values, whether your stories are strong, or whether a flagged phrase is fine in your letter. A letter can pass every check and still be forgettable, and a letter can trip a few and still be the right one. Use the results as a list of places to reread.

It also does not simulate an applicant tracking system. Cover letters are often not scored by software at all, and nobody outside an employer can say how theirs treats a letter.

The nine checks

  • Length. Words in the body, and your longest paragraph.
  • Names the employer. Whether the name you typed appears in the letter.
  • Names the role. Whether the title you typed appears, whole or in part.
  • Opening line. The first sentence, against a short list of stock openers, and whether it names the employer or role.
  • Template leftovers. Square brackets, curly braces and "company name" style placeholders.
  • Overlap with the posting. Which of the posting's repeated words your letter uses.
  • Repeated words. Everyday content words used four or more times.
  • Repeated sentence openers. The same first word starting three or more sentences.
  • Passive voice indicators. A form of "to be" followed by a past participle.

Known limits: greeting and sign-off are found by simple patterns. Sentence splitting is approximate. The passive voice rule misses some passives and flags a few adjectives. Overlap counts single words only. The stock opener list is short and in English. For the structure of a letter, see the cover letter guide.
