5. The tailored cover letter

Example, invented for illustration

This is the invented letter for the invented posting above. It names the employer and the role, and each point answers a line in the posting using a fact the student supplied. The posting gave no hiring manager's name, so the greeting does not use one.

Dear Hiring Team,

I am applying for the part-time Marketing Assistant internship at Larkspur Trail Outfitters for the spring semester. I am a business administration student at Example State College, expected to graduate in May 2027. I shop at Larkspur for hiking gear, and the monthly Saturday trail clean-up in your posting is the part I would most like to help with.

Your posting asks for flyers and signs for in-store events. In summer 2024 I made the weekly sale flyer in Canva at Greenleaf Garden Center, and the store printed it for the front window. Since September 2024 I have designed posters in Canva for campus events on the Student Activities Board.

You also mention Instagram and a spreadsheet. On the board's publicity committee I post to its Instagram account about twice a week, and in Business Statistics I used Excel to make charts.

I would be glad to talk about how I could help with the clean-up events and your social posts. Thank you for reading.

Sincerely,
Riley Sample
riley.sample@example.com

What changed and why

Each change follows a rule I apply to every pack.

  1. The two-column layout became one column. Dates moved under each job title. Rule: one column, no tables or images, so the page reads top to bottom in one order.
  2. The headings are now Education, Experience and Skills. "School" and "Work history" became the headings readers expect. Rule: standard headings.
  3. The objective is gone. It held no fact about Riley. Rule: only the facts supplied. The summary or objective guide covers when a student should use one.
  4. Duty lines became specific lines. "Responsible for the register" became "Ran the register and counted the cash drawer at close." The detail came from Riley's email. Rule: only the facts supplied.
  5. Only two numbers were added, and Riley gave both. They are "about 20 hours a week" and "about twice a week." Riley sent no follower counts or attendance figures, so the resume has none. Rule: no new numbers. The questions below ask for them.
  6. Tenses now match the dates. The current committee role uses present tense and the finished summer job uses past tense. Rule: consistent construction, a convention from the achievement bullets guide.
  7. "Hard worker," "people person" and "good with computers" were dropped. No fact in the materials backs them up, and the named programs say more. Rule: only the facts supplied.
  8. "References available upon request" was removed. It adds no fact. Rule: only the facts supplied. Dropping this line is my judgment about space, not a hard rule, and I would restore it on request.
  9. The letter answers the posting. It names Larkspur and the role, mentions the flyers, signs, trail clean-up, Instagram and spreadsheet from the posting, and ties each to something Riley said. Rule: name the employer and the role, and point to something real in the posting. The cover letter guide explains the structure.
  10. The letter claims nothing Riley did not say. Riley did not mention keeping a post calendar, so the letter does not say so. It also promises nothing about an interview. Rule: only the facts supplied, and no promises about outcomes.

What I would ask you

Example, invented for illustration

When a fact is missing, I send questions with the draft and leave the gap empty until you answer. These are the invented questions for this invented order.

  1. The posting wants a post calendar kept in a spreadsheet. Have you kept one for the board or anywhere else? If yes, tell me how it worked and I add it. If no, it stays out.
  2. Roughly how many posters have you made, and for which events? A number goes in only if you give it to me.
  3. Did the board's Instagram account gain followers or replies while you were posting? Send the before and after figures if you know them. Otherwise I leave results out.
  4. Was the Greenleaf sale flyer for a particular kind of sale or season? Name it and I add it.
  5. Do you want your GPA listed? I add it only if you send it and want it there.
  6. Do you want a city and state on the resume? I add only what you give me.
  7. Do you have a name for the hiring manager? If so, I use it in the greeting in place of "Hiring Team."
